## Summary

This randomized controlled trial examined whether structured sexual health education could improve the well-being of older women. Researchers studied 110 married women aged 65 and older in Ilam, Iran, splitting them into an intervention group of 55 and a control group of 55. The intervention consisted of six 30-minute educational sessions and a booklet, while the control group received routine care. After four weeks, the intervention group showed large, statistically significant improvements in psychological health, social well-being, and sexual self-efficacy (p < 0.001). The findings suggest that sexual health education is a valuable, underutilized tool for supporting healthy aging.
