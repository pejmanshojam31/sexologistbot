## Summary

Consent is usually taught as a way to prevent sexual violence, but this study asked whether it also relates to good sex. Among 849 adults surveyed about their most recent consensual sexual experience, internal feelings of consent — safety and comfort, arousal, and physical response — were associated with higher sexual satisfaction and pleasure, and feeling ready was associated with satisfaction. A partner's verbal consent cues, whether implicit or explicit, were also associated with both outcomes, while participants' own external communication, gender, and sexual identity were not significant predictors. The authors suggest affirmative consent could be framed not only as violence prevention but as part of sexual health promotion.
